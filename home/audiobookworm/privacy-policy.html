Audiobook Worm Privacy Policy

Effective 21 November 2026. Last updated 21 November 2026 to add optional, opt-in crash reports and usage statistics. This policy covers Audiobook Worm for Mac.

العربية

In short: Audiobook Worm builds your audiobook on your Mac. Your audio, file names and book details never leave it. There are no accounts, no ads, no tracking and no server of ours. The app connects to Apple’s iTunes Search when you ask it to look up a book, to Apple’s App Store for the one-time in-app purchase, to Google Books only if you add your own key, and to Google Firebase only if you choose to share anonymous crash reports and usage statistics. That sharing is off until you turn it on.

Who we are

Audiobook Worm is published by Timsa7 (“we”, “us”). You can reach us at support@timsa7.com.

What Audiobook Worm is

Audiobook Worm is a Mac app that merges MP3, AAC, AIFF and WAV files you choose into a single .m4b audiobook with chapter markers, embedded metadata (title, author, narrator, year, description) and cover art, ready to play in Apple Books. It does the work on your Mac. It includes no books, no catalogue and no accounts.

What stays on your Mac

Audiobook Worm keeps the following in its own storage on the Mac you use. None of it is sent to us.

  • The files you import. Your audio files, the project files (.audiobookproj) you save, and the finished .m4b files are read and written at the locations you choose. The app reaches them through security-scoped bookmarks — a macOS mechanism that grants access to specific files and folders you pick, and nothing else.
  • Settings and the free-tier export counter. Your accent colour, chosen export folder (as a security-scoped bookmark), and the number of audiobooks you’ve exported so far are stored in standard macOS preferences inside the app’s sandbox container.
  • A Google Books API key, if you add one. Stored in the same preferences store as above. Not synced anywhere.
  • Your diagnostics choice (Allow or Not Now), stored with the settings above.
  • Diagnostic logs written to macOS’s unified log via os.Logger. You can see them in the Console app or with the log command. They don’t leave your Mac, and they’re separate from the opt-in crash reports below.

Crash reports and usage statistics (only if you allow them)

The first time you open the app, a small card asks whether to share anonymous crash reports and usage statistics, with Allow and Not Now. Until you choose Allow, nothing is collected or sent. You can change your mind at any time in Settings ▸ Privacy.

If you allow it, the app uses two Google Firebase services:

  • Crash reports (Firebase Crashlytics): the stack trace and app state when the app crashes, the app version, your Mac’s model and macOS version. When an import or export fails, an error category and number (for example AVFoundation −11800), never the error message, because messages can contain file names, along with the export method and the chapter count.
  • Usage statistics (Google Analytics for Firebase): counts of actions such as files imported (how many and their formats, like “mp3”), analysis finished, cover art set, metadata look-up used, exports started, finished, failed or cancelled (with the export method, chapter count, a rough book length and a rough export time), the upgrade screen shown, and purchase or restore outcomes. Google Analytics also records standard events such as first launch, sessions and updates, and, if you buy the unlock, a purchase event with the product and its price, never your payment details.
  • A random app-instance ID created by Firebase for this installation. It isn’t your Apple ID, name, email or advertising identifier, and it’s reset if you turn sharing off.
  • Device information such as Mac model, macOS version, app version and language, and an approximate location (country or city) that Google Analytics derives from your IP address. The app never uses Location Services.

Never sent: your audio, file and folder names, and the book’s title, author, narrator, description and cover art; your name, email, Apple ID or advertising identifier. We don’t attach a user ID, link this data to you, or use it for advertising or tracking.

Google processes this data for us as a service provider under the Firebase Data Processing and Security Terms, possibly on servers outside your country. Google Analytics data sharing with Google is turned off and Google Signals is off, and the app tells Google Analytics to deny advertising storage, ad user data and ad personalization. Firebase deletes the data automatically: Google Analytics event data after 2 months, Crashlytics crash data after 90 days.

What leaves your Mac, and to whom

Audiobook Worm connects only to the following. Each sees your Mac’s IP address, as any website you visit does.

  • Apple’s iTunes Search service (itunes.apple.com). When you look up a book, or let the app look one up automatically after you import files, the title you provided (or that the app derived from the file names) is sent to Apple’s public iTunes Search service as a search query. Apple sees the request under Apple’s privacy policy. If you pick a result that has cover art, the picture is then downloaded from Apple’s image servers (is*-ssl.mzstatic.com).
  • Apple’s App Store (StoreKit). The paywall that offers the one-time Unlimited Exports purchase uses Apple’s standard in-app purchase sheet. Apple handles the payment in full. We never see your Apple ID, name, email, or payment details; Apple only tells the app whether the entitlement is active.
  • Google Books (googleapis.com), only if you add your own Google Books API key in Settings. Off by default and stays off until you add the key. If you do add it, the title you’re searching for is sent to Google as a search query, in direct response to your action. Google sees the request under Google’s privacy policy.
  • Google Firebase (firebaseinstallations.googleapis.com and the Crashlytics and Google Analytics endpoints), only if you chose Allow, as described above. Google sees the requests under Google’s privacy policy.

What we don’t do

  • No accounts, sign-ups or cloud sync.
  • No advertising, no tracking and no data brokers. Crash reports and usage statistics are sent only if you allow them.
  • One third-party SDK: Google Firebase (Crashlytics and Analytics), which stays inactive unless you allow sharing. Everything else is Apple system frameworks.
  • We don’t sell or share personal data.

If you contact us

If you email us, we receive your email address and what you write. We use them only to answer you, and we don’t share them.

Apple

Apple runs the Mac App Store and the Macs that Audiobook Worm runs on. Apple handles downloads, ratings, in-app purchase and device data under its own privacy policy. Audiobook Worm itself sends nothing to Apple beyond the iTunes Search queries and the StoreKit calls described above. The Mac App Store may show us totals, such as the number of downloads. If you’ve agreed in System Settings to share analytics with app developers, Apple may share crash reports and usage statistics with us, as that setting describes.

Children

Audiobook Worm isn’t directed at children. It collects nothing unless the person using it chooses to share anonymous crash reports and usage statistics, and those aren’t linked to a person. The app has no content of its own; what it builds is entirely from files you supply.

Removing your data

  • Turn off sharing in Settings ▸ Privacy: collection stops immediately, crash reports not yet sent are deleted, and the app-instance ID is reset.
  • Clear the Google Books API key in Settings, and no further Google Books queries are made.
  • Delete the app, and the preferences and export counter in its sandbox container go with it. Your audio files, project files and exported .m4b files stay where you saved them, outside the sandbox.

Crash reports and usage statistics aren’t linked to you, so we can’t find or delete one person’s data on request; Firebase deletes it automatically after the periods above. Apart from that, we hold nothing of yours except emails you send us.

Your rights

If you’re in the UK or the EU, you can ask us what we hold about you and have it corrected or deleted. You can also complain to a data protection authority: in the UK, the Information Commissioner’s Office (ico.org.uk).

Changes

If Audiobook Worm ever starts handling data differently, we’ll update this page and its date before the change reaches the app.

  • اختيارك بشأن التشخيص (Allow أو Not Now)، ويُحفظ مع الإعدادات المذكورة أعلاه.
  • سجلات التشخيص تُكتب في سجل macOS المُوحَّد عبر os.Logger. يمكنك الاطلاع عليها في تطبيق Console أو بأمر log، وهي لا تغادر جهاز الماك، وهي منفصلة عن تقارير الأعطال الاختيارية الموضّحة أدناه.

تقارير الأعطال وإحصاءات الاستخدام (فقط إذا سمحت بها)

عند فتح التطبيق أول مرة، تظهر بطاقة صغيرة تسألك عن مشاركة تقارير أعطال وإحصاءات استخدام مجهولة الهوية، مع زرّي Allow (السماح) وNot Now (ليس الآن). لا يُجمع شيء ولا يُرسَل حتى تختار السماح، ويمكنك تغيير رأيك في أي وقت من Settings ▸ Privacy.

إذا سمحت بذلك، يستخدم التطبيق خدمتين من Google Firebase:

  • تقارير الأعطال (Firebase Crashlytics): مسار الاستدعاءات وحالة التطبيق لحظة توقّفه المفاجئ، وإصدار التطبيق، وطراز جهاز الماك وإصدار macOS. وحين يفشل استيراد أو تصدير، تُرسَل فئة الخطأ ورقمه (مثل AVFoundation −11800) دون نص رسالة الخطأ أبداً، لأن الرسائل قد تتضمن أسماء ملفات، ومعها طريقة التصدير وعدد الفصول.
  • إحصاءات الاستخدام (Google Analytics for Firebase): أعداد إجراءات مثل استيراد الملفات (عددها وصيغها مثل «mp3»)، واكتمال التحليل، وتعيين الغلاف، واستخدام البحث عن البيانات الوصفية، وبدء التصدير أو اكتماله أو فشله أو إلغائه (مع طريقة التصدير وعدد الفصول وطول تقريبي للكتاب ومدة تصدير تقريبية)، وظهور شاشة الترقية، ونتائج الشراء أو الاستعادة. كما تسجّل Google Analytics أحداثاً قياسية مثل أول تشغيل والجلسات والتحديثات، وإذا اشتريت فتح الميزات، حدث شراء يتضمّن المنتج وسعره دون أي بيانات دفع.

ما يغادر جهاز الماك، وإلى أين

  • خدمة بحث iTunes من Apple (itunes.apple.com). حين تبحث عن كتاب، أو تسمح للتطبيق بالبحث عنه تلقائياً بعد استيراد الملفات، يُرسَل العنوان الذي قدّمته (أو الذي استخلصه التطبيق من أسماء الملفات) إلى خدمة بحث iTunes العامة كاستعلام بحث. ترى Apple الطلب وفق سياسة خصوصية Apple. وإن اخترت نتيجةً تحتوي على فن غلاف، تُحمَّل الصورة من خوادم الصور الخاصة بـ Apple (is*-ssl.mzstatic.com).
  • App Store من Apple (StoreKit). تستخدم شاشة الشراء التي تعرض عملية "التصدير غير المحدود" لمرة واحدة ورقةَ الشراء داخل التطبيق القياسية من Apple. تعالج Apple عملية الدفع كاملةً. لا نرى أبداً معرّف Apple ID الخاص بك ولا اسمك ولا بريدك الإلكتروني ولا بيانات الدفع؛ تُبلّغ Apple التطبيق فقط ما إذا كان الاستحقاق نشطاً.

  • Google Firebase (firebaseinstallations.googleapis.com وخوادم Crashlytics وGoogle Analytics)، وذلك فقط إذا اخترت السماح، كما هو موضّح أعلاه. ترى Google الطلبات وفق سياسة خصوصية Google.
